Shortcomings & examples

Blue pixels mean missing data, not fading. Mixed wavelengths can mimic brightness changes. An empty incomplete search does not mean no observations. See all limitations with examples and next steps ↗

What am I looking at?

Default frames show SPHEREx calibrated IMAGE pixels with sky alignment and a common display stretch, without zodiacal subtraction, quality-flag masking or PSF/ePSF processing. Existing background-subtracted analysis products remain available only through Advanced settings. Blue areas have no usable pixel. A common brightness stretch is used across the sequence. Target coordinates remain fixed so motion can be visible against the sky.

All-wavelength playback mixes different infrared responses. Use a narrow wavelength group for more comparable views. Groups use approximate target-position wavelengths, not a full response calibration. Coadds and differences are exploratory: PSF differences, residual astrometry, masks and noise can mimic change. These views do not establish a detection or a calibrated light curve.
